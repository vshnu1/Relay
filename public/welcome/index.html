The same reading means different things for different people.

02How it works

Four quiet steps.

Sign in once, keep wearing what you already wear, and answer a few questions when asked.

  1. 1

    Sign in

    Your discharge code, your hospital, and a yes to sharing for thirty days.

  2. 2

    Relay learns your usual

    Your watch keeps recording. Relay works out what is normal for you.

  3. 3

    It notices what moves together

    One odd reading is ignored. Several changing together, and staying changed, get a closer look.

  4. 4

    It asks you, then tells your team

    A short check-in. Your care team gets one clear summary, and a person decides what happens next.

04What to expect

What Relay will and will not do.

Relay will

  • Compare your readings with your own usual.
  • Ask you a few questions when something changes, and let you review every answer before it is sent.
  • Share only with the team that discharged you, only after you agree, and stop the moment you say so.

Relay will not

  • Say what is wrong, or grade how serious anything is. That stays with a person on your care team.
  • Send help, change your care, or message anyone else.
  • Pretend to see what it cannot. Missing readings show as not enough data, never as all clear.

Relay is not for sudden illness. It looks at patterns over days and cannot call for help. If you feel suddenly or seriously unwell, call the number on your discharge letter, or the number for immediate medical help where you live.

Nothing phones home. Even the typefaces come from Relay's own servers, a health export you bring in never leaves your browser, and every action is written to a log.

05Questions

Before you sign in.

Anything else, the number for your unit is on your discharge letter.

Do I need a smartwatch?
Relay reads what your device already records. Your hospital tells you which one to connect, and your check-ins reach your care team either way.
How often will Relay check in?
Every day for your first week home, then every other day until day thirty. A few minutes each time.
Who can see my readings?
Only the care team at the hospital that discharged you, and only after you agree. You can stop sharing at any time.
Will Relay tell me what is wrong?
No. It tells you and your team what changed and by how much. If something needs a conversation, your care team starts it.
Is Relay a real product today?
Relay is a prototype from Bay Hacks 2026. Every patient in it is synthetic, and it is not a certified medical device.
